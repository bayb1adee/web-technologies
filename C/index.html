Hegelian Dialectics: the Balmer peak as synthesis

A satirical page in the finest tradition of engineering humour.

This page is satire — the “Balmer peak” is a running joke among developers, not a productivity recommendation. The only serious part is the advice to know your limits.

The Balmer peak

Lore has it that a programmer's productivity is a function of blood alcohol. Named after the Balmer series of hydrogen spectral lines, the curve peaks around 0.129 % — the “Ballmer peak” — after which the code, like the atom's electrons, collapses to a lower energy state.

The dialectic

Hegel taught that every idea (thesis) meets its opposite (antithesis), and from their clash a higher truth (synthesis) emerges. Applied to the craft of programming:

Thesis
sober → nothing compiles
→
Antithesis
drunk → nothing compiles
→
Synthesis
the peak → ships on Friday

Three rules of culture

A. S. Makarenko, the Soviet pedagogue, argued that even here one needs culture, and offered his pupils three rules.

  1. Never drink on an empty stomach.
  2. Always eat a snack. (“Zakusyvaj!”)
  3. Know when to stop — the exact glass where you still keep a human face.
